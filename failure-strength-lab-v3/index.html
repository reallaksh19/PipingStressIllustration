ⓘ Tab 4 learning layer
Pipe-specific stress helpers · pressure design, sustained route, bending, torsion, and B31.3 paragraph map.
θHoop σθ

Concept: circumferential membrane stress from internal pressure. Piping: pressure-dominant rupture tends to split pipe longitudinally. B31.3 map: pressure-design family, especially para 304 straight-pipe pressure thickness checks; verify exact edition wording. Mistake: do not call generic σy hoop stress unless the coordinate system is cylindrical.

LAxial σL

Concept: longitudinal stress parallel to pipe axis. Piping: pressure-end, weight bending, thermal restraint, and terminal effects can contribute. B31.3 map: sustained longitudinal checks under 302.3.5 family, while pressure design remains a separate 304 route. Mistake: do not mix axial membrane, bending, and restrained thermal stress as one identical category.

rRadial σr

Concept: stress through wall thickness. Piping: often small in thin-wall teaching but real for thick-wall thinking. B31.3 map: routine checks usually use pressure-design rules; thick-wall or special cases need project review. Mistake: radial stress is neglected only when the thin-wall simplification is justified.

MBending M

Concept: longitudinal tension on one side and compression on the opposite side. Piping: spans, supports, guides, anchors, equipment loads, and settlement create bending and ovalisation cues. B31.3 map: sustained, occasional, or displacement range depending on source. Mistake: do not combine bending with pressure using one universal pass/fail route.

τTorsion τt

Concept: twisting shear around the pipe axis. Piping: eccentric loads, equipment movement, branches, and constrained rotations may generate torsion. B31.3 map: include it in the relevant stress route according to load case and verify against the licensed edition. Mistake: do not label torsion as pressure hoop stress or generic τxy without coordinate definition.
